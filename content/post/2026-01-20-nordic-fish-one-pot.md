## Intro

Growing up, I was always told we ate European-style: late, after 7PM, sometimes even stretching after 8. This became ingrained in me, and combined with my night-owl tendencies, dinner frequently became a pre-bed snack.

You can imagine my surprise when I learned [the northern] half of Europe does not adhere to these standards[^1]:

![Dinner Times](/img/cooking/fish-one-pot/dinner_times.png)

True to form, Ida usually reports "starving" status by about 5:30PM. By the time I finish cooking, it's 7PM at the earliest (and the snacks are gone). Hearing my loyal customer, I have begun an initiative for 30-minute *max* meals, from ingredients laid out to eating.

I found this recipe in the archives of my Facebook DMs[^2]. It promised a fast meal, so I decided to give it a chance. We rarely eat white fish anymore, even though it is prevalent in the area, so this looked like a fun fusion of Nordic and southern European flavors.

## Ingredients
- `1.5 lbs` white fish (~750 g)
- `2 cups` risoni/orzo (~450 mL)
- `1 can` cannellini/navy beans (400 g)
- `1 cup` kalamata olives (~225 mL)
- `1 cup` cherry tomatoes (~225 mL)
- `2 tbsp` tomato paste (~30 mL)
- `1` lemon (juice and zest)
- `1/2` yellow onion
- `1 tbsp` chicken fond/bouillon (~15 mL) + `2 cups` water (~450 mL)
  - *Note: can substitute chicken stock*
- `1 cup` water (~225 mL)
- `1 tbsp` paprika (~15 mL)
- `1 tbsp` dried oregano (~15 mL)
- `1 tbsp` salt
- `1 tsp` black pepper (~5 mL)
- `1 tbsp` olive oil
- Fresh parsley
- `3 cloves` garlic (optional)

![Ingredient Spread](/img/cooking/fish-one-pot/ingredient_spread.jpeg)

I used an extremely mysterious nordic white fish known as a cusk in English, or brosme in Norwegian/Swedish? (Ida had never heard of either name). I would have preferred to use a cheaper alternative like frozen cod, but I shopped late and needed a fresh option, so cusk it was.

I have really embraced using fond extracts in Sweden, as they are ubiquitous here and come in a variety of flavors. Chicken stock works equally as well, but when you have to carry your groceries home, it is among the first things to cut.

## Directions
`Timer: 30:00` 

Begin coarsely dicing the half of the onion. If using garlic, finely dice (or press) three cloves and add everything to a large stockpot at medium-high heat with the olive oil. Stir occasionally to prevent burning.

![Onion Garlic](/img/cooking/fish-one-pot/onion_garlic.jpeg)

`Timer: 25:00` 

Cut 10-12 cherry tomatoes in half lengthwise. Add to the pot along with the tomato paste, continuously stirring. 
Add the risoni, water, and fond/stock into the mix. There should be 3 cups (~675 mL) of liquid in the pot now.

Zest the lemon directly over the pot and squeeze in 1/2 of the juice. Add in the oregano and mix.

Drain the 20-30 olives and stir into the pot. Lower the heat to medium. If all goes well, the base should be looking similar to this:

![Pot Base](/img/cooking/fish-one-pot/pot_base.jpeg)

`Timer: 20:00` 

While the pot is simmering, pat dry and season both sides of the fish generously with the salt, pepper, and paprika. Cut into 3" (7.5 cm) sections. Catch your breath for one second.

`Timer: 15:00`

Lay the fish pieces atop of the base. Quickly wash the cutting board and coarsely chop some of the parsley and sprinkle over the top of the fish. Here is a very steamy status update:

![Fish in Pot](/img/cooking/fish-one-pot/fish_in_pot.jpeg)

`Timer: 10:00`

Flip each pieces once. Open the can of beans, drain, and pour evenly over the top of the fish fillets. Lemon lovers can add another squeeze over the top of everything now. Lower the heat to medium-low.

`Timer: 5:00`
Use some discretion with the timer based on your fish variety, but when it is flaking easily, turn off the burner. 

My pot had a lot of residual fluid at this point, so use a slotted spoon to scoop out the fish and base. Serve hot with more diced parsley and lemon zest over the top. Salt to taste.

`Timer: 0:00`

Eat! Enjoy a low effort, gourmet meal that pairs flaky fish with a delicious Mediterranean base.

### - Summary

This a unique blend of flavors and textures. I have eaten a lot of halibut in my life, and this would be a phenomenal dish for it if you are willing to splurge. I never cook with olives, and both Ida and I agreed they were a fantastic counter to the fish, refreshing the palate with wash of vinegar. Ida did not like the beans, and they were admittedly a bit strange with the fish texture, but I found it to be surprisingly pleasant.

The cusk fish was a rollercoaster in itself. Probably the sharpest bones I have ever had in a fish filet, and the meat was thick like lobster or maybe even chicken. I googled a photo [^3] of it after and they are some real deep-water freaksters:

![Brosme Fish](/img/cooking/fish-one-pot/brosme_fish.jpg)

The dish achieved the 30-minute goal, and clean-up was minimal. All in all, definitely worth a try if you want to spice up your weeknight dinners.
